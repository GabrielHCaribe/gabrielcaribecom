Overview

Blue Ocean Strategy by W. Chan Kim and Renée Maes gives actionable frameworks for building a company strategy that exits the "red ocean" of bloody competition, and enters a "blue ocean" where you dominate the market by creating your own market.

General themes from the book: make a strategy canvas to know the state of the industry, then do ......

Value Innovation: when actions favorably affect cost sstructre and value propositions

  • Cost savings from eliminating facots an industry competes on
  • Increase buyer value by creating elements industry never offered

Blue Ocean: differentiation AND lower costs; looks like when you can say "Not a traditional (...) (eg. Circus)

How to Formulate a Blue Ocean Strategy

  1. Reconstruct market boundaries
  2. Focus on Big Picture, not numbers
  3. Reach beyond existing demand
  4. Get the strategy sequence right

"How do I make competition irrelevant?"

* Strategy Canvas

  • Diagnoses the playing field, shows you where competition is investing and what theyre competing on
  • Is an actionable framework

To shift strategy canvas, change strategy focus from competitors to alternatives, and from customers to noncustomers of the industry.

4 Actions Framework

  • Reduce: Which factors should be reduced well below industry standards
  • Create: Which factors should be created that the industry has never offered
  • Raise: Which factors should be raised well above industry standards
  • Eliminate: Which of the factors the industry takes for granted should be eliminated

Answering all of these leads to a new value curve

The labels on your strategy canvas should be in terms buyers understand and value

* 6 Paths Framework (Reconstructing Market Boundaries)

Path 1: Look across alternative industries

Alternaties example: restauraunts vs. movie theatres - both are entertainment, each offers value to different people.

Ask Yourself:

  • What are the alternatives to my industry
  • What do customers trade across them? (Find the best parts and reduce or eliminate everything else)

Path 2: Look across strategic groups within your industry

Strategic groups are generally ranked in higherarchical order baesd on price and performance

  • Ex. luxury vs. everyday cars

Ask Yourself:

  • What are strategic groups in my industry and why do customers trade/change?

Path 3: Look across the chain of buyers

Explain a bit and give the example

Ask Yourself:

  • How could designing for a different buyer change the product to be more appealing?

Path 4: Look across complimentary products and service offerings

Explain a bit and give the example

Ask Yourself:

  • What is the context my product is used?
  • What happens before, during, and after?
  • Find pain points and eliminate them

Path 5: Look across functional or emotional appeal to buyers

Explain a bit and give the example

Ask Yourself:

  • Is my industry functional or emotional and what elements can I switch to be the opposite?

Path 6: Look across time

Look for decisive, irreversible trends that have a clear trajectory (give example)

Then, look into the future and ask what would the market look like if the trend were taken to its logical conclusion

Ask Yourself:

  • How will these trends impact my industry, and how can I use this to open up unprecedented customer utility?

2. Focusing on the Big Picture

Process for Drawing and Discussing Strategy Canvas

  1. Visual Awakening:
    • Draw the strategy canvas for your business and competitors "as is"
  2. Visual Exploration
    • Go into the field to explore the 6 paths to creating blue oceans
    • Observe advantages of alternative products and services
    • Decide what to eliminate, create, or change
  3. Visual Strategy Fair
    • Draw your to-be strategy canvas
    • Get feedback on it from customers, competitors' customers, and non-customers
    • incorperate the feedback
  4. Visual Communication
    • Distribute before and after strategy profiles on one page for comparison
    • Only support projects that close the gap to your new strategy
